We thank the referee for the precise distinction between demonstrated errors,
checked arguments and unreplayed computations. This revision also follows the
author's instruction to distribute a compact companion: apart from manuscript
TeX/PDF files, the total size of all accompanying material is below 10 MB. Large certificate inventories are
omitted rather than divided into smaller archive parts.

\section*{R1: certificate monotonicity}
The assertion that enlarging a necessary row set preserves every negative
certificate was false. The revised statement and proof use the correct
direction: a certificate verified on a complete necessary superset applies
to every subset. Enlargement requires a fresh check against every new row;
a bounded certificate must include the bounds for all new negative
contributions. The counterexample with rows $(1,0)$, $(1,1)$, target $(1,1)$
and covector $(0,-1)$ makes the distinction explicit.

The same unsafe direction was also removed from the central-spectrum
and six-form discussions. The compact companion includes a regression check
for this counterexample.
Such a check validates the corrected elementary rule; it does not certify
coverage of an old field catalogue. We do not infer validity of historical
exclusions from their aggregate counts or from the correction of this sentence.

\section*{R2: missing computations and complete coverage}
The report reviewed the PDF without its large computational companion. The
prepared Git bundle did contain a companion, but the present delivery follows
the subsequent instruction to remove large verification files. Consequently,
we do not claim to have fulfilled the request for a complete independently
replayed large certificate package.

Instead, the manuscript now distinguishes proved theoretical statements,
bounded standalone calculations, and numerical conclusions conditional on
complete coverage and valid exact certificates. In particular, the stronger
source-rank and canonical-length bounds and the global remaining-state
inventory are not presented as certified by the compact checks. Assertions
of uniqueness inside that inventory require the same qualification. The
explicit thirteen-row obstruction for the specified assignment is separate
from the assertion that this is the only assignment left by a global screen.

The current verification guide and machine-readable scope map identify
which calculations can actually be run. Retained historical source code is
provided as source only; omitted inputs and witnesses are not reconstructed
by reading that archive. A completed small calculation, an input count,
a hash match or an arithmetic subtraction does not validate a larger
exhaustive exclusion. Failed or unfinished searches give no exclusion.

This is a substantive narrowing of what the compact release claims to
verify. No mathematical counterexample to the stated category bounds is
asserted. Restoring their unconditional computational status would require
complete necessary-input generation, exact witness checking and coverage
verification, including the additional fields, involutions, repeated roots
and dimension ranges identified by the referee.

\section*{R3: weakly integral anomaly}
The weakly integral step now cites Dong--Lin--Ng, Theorem 6.10. Proposition
6.7(ii) is reserved for the integral situation. The anomaly and the scalar
normalization of a linear modular representation are distinguished, and the
minimal-extension theorem is not credited with establishing integrality
without the additional argument.

\section*{R4: matroid-bound variable}
The expression $4n-14$ in the two-component estimate has been corrected to
$4\nu-14$. The endpoint computation with component sizes adding to $\nu$
is unchanged. This is a local typographical correction.

\section*{R5 and the old--new comparison}
A provenance table identifies inherited statements, strengthened statements,
and different arguments. In particular, the conductor framework, even
frontier, first odd numerical frontier and degree-six conclusion are not
claimed as new relative to the earlier joint draft. The block decomposition,
stronger local/support methods and commutator/reconstruction theory are
identified separately. A different proof is not a new numerical conclusion;
a comparison of two drafts is not a literature-wide priority claim.

We also record the comparison's combined modular rank consequence.
Theorem C(i) removes the missing-$6$ and missing-$10$ configurations
from Theorem 29.1 of the earlier joint draft, giving rank at least
$106$, or $130$ without a dimension-$900$ simple. A thirty-divisible
twist gives the stronger bounds $112$ and $142$. The revised
introduction displays the numerical degree budgets and their rounding.
The needed full-graph degree budgets do not use the omitted
source-certificate archives, so this consequence is separate from
verification hypothesis $\mathsf V$.

The present manuscript is a focused research draft, not a replacement for
the earlier general dimension/rank manuscript. The earlier manuscript and
its broader results remain separately available in the repository. The
comparison's list of omitted general results therefore does not justify
silently attributing them to this focused text or deleting their original
source. All theoretical exposition of the present draft remains in its main
sections.

\section*{Checks and remaining limits}
The distributed checks and their actual results are described in
\texttt{verification/README.md}. Both manuscripts have been typeset after
revision; the package includes their source files, revision responses and
integrity manifests. These are selective mathematical and computational
checks, not a proof-assistant certification or an exhaustive independent
refereeing of every argument in the long draft.

The dimension $5400^2$ remains unresolved. Likewise, necessary supports at
the odd scalar frontier do not constitute a modular datum, fusion ring or
fusion category. The revision makes no new existence claim.
