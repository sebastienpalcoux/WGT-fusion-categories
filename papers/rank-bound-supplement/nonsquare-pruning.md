# Nonsquare dimensions at rank 31

At rank 31, a largest degree of codegree eight has multiplicity at most four, and at most two if its degree is even. Here the global dimension is 2M² and that degree is x=M/2. The joint prime bound and Lemma 4.1 of the rank-bound paper force the prime support to be exactly {2,3,5,7}. For even x, the proof of Lemma 4.1 shows that every x-row takes the same four rational coordinates on the odd labels: the vacuum value 1 and three signs whose signed degree sum is −1. The signs are fixed: one degree must differ by one from the sum of the other two, and that degree is the unique largest since each is at least six. The normalized rows are orthonormal. Each has squared projection 1/2 onto their common unit sign vector supported on the four odd labels, so there can be at most two. For odd x, an x-row must hit a degree-x column: otherwise all nonunit hit degrees divide x properly and are at most x/3, contradicting the modular inequality √8 x ≤ 1+7x/3. Symmetry and principal support thus put each x-row in the principal dyadic block, where it hits every odd degree. The hit degrees have nonunit gcd one and each has at least two prime divisors. At least three proper odd hit degrees are necessary because any two mixed-prime divisors supported on {3,5,7} have a common prime. There are at most eight hits by the row norm, including the vacuum, all maximal labels, and these three proper ones. This gives the bound of four. The code uses that 4|M whenever 4 divides the lcm of a prefix.

For D=3M², the labels whose denominators z=M/d are prime to three have ternary defect one, i.e. v₃(D/d²)=1. Their degree multiset partitions into triples (r,r,r) and blocks (r,r,2r). This is the ternary case of Proposition 19.11 and Corollary 19.12 in Burciu–Dong–Palcoux, *Prime factors of fusion-category dimensions: weak group-theoreticity, rank and decomposition*, Hexagon 2611.00233, revised version, “Partition of the defect-one degrees”. In brief, each defect-one block consists of two tame inertia orbits of defect one and lengths a,b in {1,2}. The local inertia orbit of larger phase order is the order-three orbit, and the other has trivial ternary phase. If their degrees are r,s, respectively in the inactive and active orbits, the complementary phase identity gives b s²=2a r². The integer solutions for orbit lengths are (a,b,s/r)=(1,2,1) and (2,1,2), giving the two stated possibilities. This argument uses the local structure of the defect-one block, independently of any results about families of global dimensions.

In denominators these blocks are (z,z,z) and (z,2z,2z). Read the sorted denominators in increasing order. At each 3-free z reserve the copies previously required by z/2. If n copies remain, use n mod 3 copies to start doubled blocks at 2z and form triples with the rest. This greedy choice is exact: three additional doubled blocks can always be replaced by one triple at z and two triples at 2z, leaving every multiplicity intact. An obligation missed by a completed run, or requiring more labels than the suffix contains, rules out the prefix. The two terminal denominators are checked before a full list is emitted.

## A local proof of the ternary block statement

Complete the fusion ring at 3 and extend its unramified coefficient ring O until residue fields split. In a fixed local block A the generic factors are totally ramified cyclotomic fields Eᵢ. All their coordinates on elements of A have the same residue. The coefficient-of-unit trace is the sum of field traces Trᵢ/fᵢ, and its dual order is A. Write eᵢ and δᵢ for the ramification index and different exponent. The character different bound gives δᵢ≤eᵢ v₃(fᵢ).

If v₃(fᵢ)=1, the branch is tame: a wild branch has different exponent at least (eᵢ−1)+(3−1)>eᵢ by the first two ramification groups. Thus eᵢ=1 or 2. There is another branch j, since otherwise the idempotent 1 of this block would have nonintegral trace eᵢ/fᵢ. On the integers of Eᵢ the map λᵢ=Trᵢ/eᵢ reduces to the residue. On Oⱼ choose an O-linear residue lift λⱼ, for example the constant coefficient in a uniformizer power basis. Write λⱼ=Trⱼ(c·). Since λⱼ carries the maximal ideal into 3O and takes some unit value, the definition of the inverse different gives vⱼ(c)=eⱼ−δⱼ−1.

The difference (λᵢ−λⱼ)/3 is integral on A. Self-duality puts its representing element in A. Its i-coordinate fᵢ/(3eᵢ) is a unit, its j-coordinate is −fⱼc/3 and all other coordinates are zero. Equality of residues therefore excludes every third branch and forces the j-coordinate to be a unit. Consequently v₃(fⱼ)=(δⱼ+1)/eⱼ. A wild abelian branch of index eⱼ=u3ˢ (u=1 or 2, s≥1) would give (δⱼ+1)/eⱼ=s+1−(1−3⁻ˢ)/2, strictly between successive integers. Indeed the local cyclotomic extension of level 3ˢ⁺¹ contains it with tame index 2/u after unramified base change. Its normalized different is s+1−1/2 by the cyclotomic derivative, and transitivity of differents gives the displayed formula. Thus Eⱼ is tame as well and v₃(fⱼ)=1. The two branches yield two inertia orbits of length one or two.

The blockwise odd-valuation twist-mass identity and the twist-order bound now give one inactive orbit (length a, degree r) and one active orbit (length b, degree s), with b s²=2a r². In {1,2} only (a,b,s/r)=(1,2,1) and (2,1,2) occur. This proves the two allowed degree multisets independently of the dimension-family results.
