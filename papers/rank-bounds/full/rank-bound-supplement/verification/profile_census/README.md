# Odd-square prime profiles

Run from the supplement root with `python -B verification/profile_census/check.py`. Add `--regenerate-graphs` to generate the graph reservations from the filtered prime starts and compare each tuple, in order, with its indexed entry in the archived list. The default reconstructs the prime starts and compares the resulting index map with this supplement's receipt. It reads the two source-bound auxiliary lists and their arithmetic source digests from the pinned neutral-orbit provenance manifest. Both modes leave those files unchanged.

The ceilings 201 and 209 use odd square dimension, and rank is one modulo eight. A prime start specifies its complete dimension support and the complete nonzero prime-denominator classes. The class theorem requires valuation one, multiplicity $pj$, and active multiplicity $j(p-1)$, with $j$ a proper divisor of $p-1$. In odd dimension $2^{v_2(p-1)}\mid j$, since the neutral orbit has size $j$ and has nonreal field in the cyclic $p$-cyclotomic extension. The small-prime results exclude denominator three, five or seven. The checker applies these rules before counting. Within these ceilings the individual restrictions in the source-bound comparison produce the same class shapes.

The additional simultaneous test uses the following bounds.

- The ternary-edge theorem permits only five as a neighbor of three. For two primes at least five, the odd-edge cost excludes every pair besides five and seven at these ceilings. For five and seven it requires 172 denominators divisible by 35. Even granting every other position floor nine gives mass at most $172/35^2+37/9^2<1$. Thus only the empty graph and the single edge between three and five need be tried.
- At an isolated marked prime the complete class costs $m$ positions at $p$. Its field population is a multiple of $\max(8,2d)$, with $d=2^{v_2(p-1)}$. At minimal activity it is exactly $2p+2$. Remaining positions in that field have denominators divisible by $p$, different from $p$, and hence at least $p\min P$.
- An absent isolated prime supplies $d(p+1)$ positions whose denominators are at least $p^2$. Below $2p(p-1)$ active labels, the uniform component and sparse-height bounds put every member of the field component at this floor. Above that threshold the pure active labels alone suffice. The component population has the same modulus. When $R-1<2p(p-1)$, the sparse floor applies to all its additional positions as well.
- A mixed ternary-quinary component supplies eighty positions at denominator at least fifteen, disjoint from the complete class at nine. All its other component positions round to a total multiple of eight. This follows because all isolated component populations and $r-1$ are multiples of eight.
- Different components are disjoint. The minimum denominator of any unbooked label, before closing the class at nine, is at least $(\min P)^2$. Closing its even multiplicity, at most 76, raises that floor to at least fifteen. In an isolated ternary field the complete class at nine credits the existing component bound. An isolated prime absent as a denominator has height at least two. On three-prime support every height is at least two, excluding all marked primes. These give the conservative vacuum divisor used in the test. Unused slots are assigned to the smallest lower floor of any field with open population. The comparison with mass one uses integer squares of the common multiple of the floors.
- A start survives when some graph and some allowed nine multiplicity pass. At the reservation stage the archived graph and nine multiplicity are fixed. The per-ceiling receipt lists the saved zero-based indices, in the saved order. Neither a start nor a retained reservation asserts realizability.
